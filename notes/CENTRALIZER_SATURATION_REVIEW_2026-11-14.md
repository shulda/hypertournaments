## Exact mathematical assumptions

`centralizer_saturation` assumes groups `E`, `K`, finite `K`, an action
`MulDistribMulAction E K`, `IsPGroup 2 E`, and odd `Nat.card K`. It takes
elements `a`, `b` with `a*a = b*b = 1`, `Commute a b`, and
`a != 1`, `b != 1`, `a*b != 1`. It takes a subgroup `H`, its elementwise
invariance under every scalar, and the assertion that every point fixed by
each nonidentity scalar belongs to `H`. Its conclusion is `H = top`.

In particular, it does **not** assume that `E` is finite, that the action is
faithful, that `E` is abelian, that `K` is nontrivial, or that `H` is normal.
The displayed `a`, `b` generate a Klein subgroup. For a larger acting group,
the fixed-point hypothesis ranges over all its nonidentity elements.
This is a valid generalization of the preparation's Klein-group statement.
Mathlib's `IsPGroup` means that each element has prime-power order; its
fixed-point theorem used here genuinely permits infinite acting groups.

`nonidentity_fixed_elements_generate` has the same group/action/Klein-element
assumptions and concludes that the subgroup generated by
`{x | exists e, e != 1 and e • x = x}` is all of `K`.

## Restriction and quotient actions

`stableSubgroupAction` defines the scalar map on the actual subtype `H` as
`x |-> e • x`, with membership supplied by the given invariance proof.
Its action and multiplication laws are inherited through `Subtype.ext`.
It does not replace the action by an arbitrary existing instance.

`stable_normalizer` uses Mathlib's actual normalizer membership criterion.
For forward preservation it tests an arbitrary `y` by first applying the
inverse scalar, conjugating by the original normalizing element, and then
applying the scalar. The reverse direction applies the forward result to
the inverse scalar. Thus the normalizer is stable under the same action.

`stableQuotientAction` proves the actual `MulAction.QuotientAction`
congruence: membership of `x^-1*y` in `H` implies membership of
`(e • x)^-1*(e • y)` in `H`. This requires only stability, not normality.
For normal `H`, `stableQuotientDistribAction` upgrades this descended action
using the surjective homomorphism `QuotientGroup.mk' H` and its actual
equivariance equation. Its representative action is definitionally
`e • [x] = [e • x]`.

Independent Lean examples checked both the subtype scalar formula and this
quotient representative formula by `rfl` under the exported instances.

## Saturation proof, step by step

1. If `H` is proper, its finite index is greater than one. The chosen prime
   `p` divides this index. The proof uses this index rather than an unrelated
   subgroup order, and installs `Fact p.Prime` from the chosen witness.
2. The invariant-Sylow interface returns an actual `Sylow p K` with the
   original action preserving membership. If `P <= H`,
   `Subgroup.index_dvd_of_le` gives `[K:H]` dividing `[K:P]`; this contradicts
   `P.not_dvd_index`. The direction of index divisibility is correct.
3. On the actual group `P`, the subgroup
   `J = H.comap P.subtype` consists exactly of the elements of `P` whose
   underlying element lies in `H`. Thus `J` represents `P intersect H`,
   inherits stability from `H`, and is proper by the preceding step.
4. The finite `p`-group `P` is nilpotent. The imported normalizer condition
   gives the strict inclusion `J < N_P(J)`, not merely containment.
   The proof sets `N` to this actual normalizer and obtains its stability
   from `stable_normalizer`.
5. `M = J.subgroupOf N` is the copy of `J` inside `N`. Its properness follows
   from the strict normalizer inclusion via `subgroupOf_eq_top`.
   `Subgroup.normal_in_normalizer` supplies its normality. No normality of
   `H` in `K` or of `J` in `P` is inserted.
6. `Q = N / M` is finite and nontrivial. Its automorphism action is precisely
   the quotient action above. The Klein fixed-element lemma gives a
   nonidentity scalar `e` with `e*e = 1` and a fixed nonidentity quotient
   element. The lemma requires neither odd order of `Q` nor faithfulness.
7. Choosing a representative `k : N`, quotient equality yields exactly
   `k^-1*(e • k) in M`. This is invariance of the left coset `kM`.
   The scalar on `N` is involutive by `e*e = 1`.
8. Oddness descends successively from `K` to `P`, `N`, and `M` by subgroup
   cardinal divisibility. The odd-coset lemma supplies `x : N` in `kM`
   fixed by the actual scalar automorphism of `N`. It requires no ambient
   normality or extra finite ambient hypothesis.
9. Coercing its fixed-point equality through `N -> P -> K` allows application
   of `hfixed`. Therefore the underlying point is in `H`; because `x` lies
   in `N <= P`, this says exactly `x in M`.
   Combining `k^-1*x in M` with `x in M` gives `k in M`, so its quotient
   class is the identity, contradicting the chosen quotient element.

The generation corollary proves stability of its generating set explicitly.
If `d` fixes `x`, then `e*d*e^-1` fixes `e • x`; the conjugate remains
nonidentity. Closure induction then gives stability of the generated
subgroup. This works for nonabelian `E`; the preparation's commutative
Klein argument is a special case. No centralizer-generation theorem is
assumed as an input.

## Supporting interfaces

`InvariantSylow.lean` bounds the number of Sylow subgroups by divisibility
into `Nat.card K` using `P.card_dvd_index` and `P.index_dvd_card`.
It installs `Sylow.pointwiseMulAction` for the original automorphism action,
then applies
`IsPGroup.nonempty_fixed_point_of_prime_not_dvd_card` to that finite Sylow
set. A fixed Sylow subgroup is converted back to elementwise invariance
using `Subgroup.smul_mem_pointwise_smul_iff`. Inspection of the pinned
Mathlib statement confirms no finite-`E` hypothesis.

`KleinQuotientLift.lean` constructs a genuine equivalence from any Sylow
`p`-subgroup of finite `L` to a `p`-group quotient `E` when the kernel's
order is prime to `p`. Surjectivity uses `Sylow.mapSurjective` and
maximality of a Sylow subgroup in a `p`-group. Injectivity proves the
intersection with the kernel trivial by the incompatible cardinal
divisibility conditions. The equivalence's point map is exactly the
given quotient homomorphism on the subgroup, with an `rfl` application
lemma. It is not an abstract isomorphism with an unrelated map.

The order-four wrapper requires `Nat.card E = 4` and odd kernel. Its
conclusion is valid for both kinds of order-four group, including cyclic
ones. It does not assert that every order-four group is Klein or supply
the elements `a`, `b` required by saturation. In the EPPA application those
elements must come from the specified Klein quotient; this is the intended
interface boundary, not a missing provider in the group theorem.
